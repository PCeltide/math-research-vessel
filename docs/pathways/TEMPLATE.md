## Question and payoff

State the question, setting, objects and relevant notion of equality. What uncertainty would either a positive or negative answer resolve? How would it help the central investigation? If there is an article, what reader need might it serve without promising admission?

## Origin and decision history

Record the proposer, date and discussion or exact source/claim locator. Distinguish an agent recommendation from researcher selection. Explain why chosen or deferred, credible alternatives, and significant changes of formulation. Label summaries as summaries.

## Known inputs and open premises

Identify inherited results with exact revisions and review coverage. Separate accepted inputs, unresolved premises, unread source leads and speculative mechanisms. Record recovery information for inputs needed by another session, respecting private-source storage rules.

## First decisive test

Describe a bounded example, comparison, construction or obstruction. What would justify continuing? What would falsify the proposed route? What would not count as progress? A useful synthesis, refined question or precise obstruction can be a legitimate endpoint.

## Routes, dependencies and risks

Compare the useful routes, their purpose, required inputs/tools, estimated effort and exit conditions. Identify scope changes requiring a new decision. State the risk of duplicating known work or losing the intended payoff, with a focused positioning question if useful.

## Proposed assignment boundary

Describe the smallest useful deliverable, exact inputs, exclusions, effort bound, output locations and required review. Link a separate executable plan when prepared; this card never launches itself or supersedes a launched plan.

## Outcome and reconsideration

Once investigated, link the canonical result and exact review state; explain what was settled and what remains open. Record conditions for reopening, retiring or splitting the idea. Keep a possible article role distinct from acceptance or admission.

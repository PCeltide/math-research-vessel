# Research pathways

A pathway preserves why a question is worth asking, what progress would look like, and how another session could begin. Use this collection when an idea should outlive a conversation. A short ledger entry is enough at first; a card is useful when its reasoning or restart details would be costly to reconstruct. No subject-specific directions come with the vessel.

The [policy](../collaboration.md), charter and launched assignment govern execution. A pathway is neither a proof nor permission to start research.

## Four priority buckets

| Bucket | Meaning | Reason to reconsider |
|---|---|---|
| **Next** | A researcher-selected direction to prepare or pursue under an explicit launch | Preparation establishes readiness; the launch establishes authority |
| **Worth returning to** | A promising question with a concrete payoff and first test | New evidence, a closed dependency or a priority decision |
| **Conditional or tangential** | Useful if a stated obstacle, reader need or change of direction arises | Its named trigger occurs |
| **Settled or retired** | A bounded question is completed, superseded, combined with another, or declined with a reason | A named new fact justifies reopening |

Priority, execution, evidence and article selection are separate judgments. Keep the workflow's dispositions (open, active, completed, parked, retained) and record readiness where useful: captured, prepared, launched or closed. A prepared plan can remain unlaunched. Label inherited results, unchecked leads and conjectural routes individually with their evidence states. If the project has an article, explain the possible role of a result; admission remains a separate decision under the [article guide](../guides/central-article.md).

When recommending a direction, compare its contribution to the central question, the first decisive test, dependencies, likely effort and risk of repeating known work. Give reasons for choosing and deferring alternatives. Record a preferred order with its date and whether it is an agent recommendation or researcher decision. It is revisable, not an automatic execution queue. Interesting tangents need not be described as central to be retained.

## One home for each kind of information

| Record | Owns |
|---|---|
| `docs/ledger.md` | Current priority, disposition/readiness, short rationale, next dependency or trigger, and links |
| `docs/pathways/Wnnn-<name>.md` | Motivation, precise candidate questions, routes, uncertainties, restart inputs and dated decision history |
| `docs/plans/<task>.md` | The bounded executable assignment, exact inputs, review gates and launch authority |
| `docs/reports/<task>.md`, note and evidence | Results or obstructions, complete reviews and exact identities |
| Article narrative/selection record, when used | Whether and how a result serves the article's current argument |

Create the ledger on first need. Use stable IDs such as W001; keep an existing project's IDs and never reuse a retired one. A practical ledger table has columns for ID/link, bucket, disposition/readiness, question/payoff, first check/dependencies, and dated rationale. Include effort and risks in the row or linked card. Keep current priorities here rather than duplicating a live board in each card. No empty project ledger or example research claim is required by this template.

## Capture, prepare, launch, reconcile

**Capture without diverting.** During authorized work, retain a promising idea with its question, origin or exact locator, possible payoff, evidence state, first check, dependency and reason it lies outside the current assignment. A few sentences suffice. Record unclear questions as unclear; do not run a side investigation merely to fill a card. A proof gap in active work belongs in that work's report and status, not in a parked pathway.

**Expand when useful.** Copy [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) to a card when the idea is selected, likely to be resumed, or costly to reconstruct. Preserve why alternatives were deferred, exact input revisions and coverage, and a legitimate partial endpoint. Preparation may expose an ill-posed or already answered question. Do not promise a theorem or claim novelty from an unsuccessful search.

**Prepare and launch explicitly.** Translate a selected card into the [workflow's preparation packet](../guides/workflow.md#prepare-the-next-assignment) and bounded plan. Apply the existing review gates to a substantial prepared assignment and its repairs; casual idea capture does not require independent review. A card, a preferred order and a review PASS do not launch work. Record the researcher's actual instruction and identify the plan/input revisions when launched. A future session follows AGENTS startup, the handoff and complete coverage, and checks actual checkout ownership and identities before continuing. Later card edits never silently expand a launched assignment.

**Reconcile at real checkpoints.** At assignment closeout, a direction discussion or a dependency-changing result, update the affected ledger entries and card histories without waiting for another reminder. Is the question still open? Does it still help the central investigation? Has a dependency closed or an article need changed? Record bucket changes with dated reasons. Combine duplicate ideas through cross-references retaining both IDs. Retire stale ideas with a reason and reopening condition rather than deleting their history. Read only the relevant part of the collection; this is not scheduled or background research.

**Return evidence.** Link a completed investigation's report, exact result scope and full review coverage back to its card. Say what it settled and what it did not. A broad theme may generate several bounded investigations; a new follow-up gets a separate candidate or clearly identified continuation and the appropriate launch. Research can be worthwhile without contributing article content.

This guide organizes the existing workflow. It grants no new research, merge, publication or cleanup authority. Projects may adapt the naming and detail to their needs while retaining their governing policy and evidence requirements.
